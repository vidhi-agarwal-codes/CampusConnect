From profile to roster in four steps

The same profile you fill out once gets weighed against every project on campus looking for your skill set.

i.

Build your profile

List your skills, interests, past project experience, weekly availability, and the domains you want to work in.

ii.

Post your open roles

Starting a project? Define what it needs — say, one back-end developer, one UI/UX designer, one database developer, one DSA specialist.

iii.

Review your matches

Campus Connect ranks students against each role and shows why they fit — shared skills, overlapping availability, relevant domain.

iv.

Lock in your team

Send invites, confirm roles, and start building with a roster that's balanced instead of whoever replied first.

What goes into a match

No single factor decides a recommendation. Campus Connect weighs all six together, per role.

Skills

Languages, frameworks, and tools a student has actually used, not just listed.

Interests

The kinds of problems and products a student wants to work on right now.

Experience

Prior coursework, hackathons, and shipped projects relevant to the role.

Availability

Weekly hours a student can commit, checked against the project's pace.

Preferred domain

Whether the student wants to be building in, say, health, fintech, or gaming.

Required role

The specific seat a project is trying to fill, matched to who can actually fill it.

What a finished match looks like

A project posts four roles. Campus Connect returns a ranked candidate for each, with the overlap that earned the recommendation.
